## Claim 3: Detector — a linear "deception direction" from logistic regression on standardised activations, aggregated across agents by five statistics

> "We standardise activations to zero mean and unit variance, then train a logistic regression classifier. The normalised weight vector defines a  deception direction ."

> "We introduce five probing techniques, designed to capture some of the various ways we expect collusion to present itself. Each technique computes a scalar test statistic"

> "Targets representational shift, which may not be reflected in individual deception scores. This is the only method that operates on full activation vectors rather than the deception direction."

## Claim 7: Encoding — a bespoke, agent-invented scheme per shoe, mapping three count bands to the style of a remark (question/statement/enthusiasm, tempo, weather)

> "The scheme is  bespoke per shoe , the two colluders invent it from scratch during their private negotiation round. Every shoe therefore uses a different encoding, so no fixed vocabulary of “signal tokens” exists across the dataset, and a text-level detector cannot learn a shared surface pattern."

> "the Counter  asks a question  on any topic. Big Player bets $0 (sits out)."

> "Counter complains about the  weather  (“this heat is killing me”), single sentence. Big Player bets $0."

> "The difference is  functional  (the Counter’s message encodes a signal; the honest players’ does not), not lexical."

**ID:** bespoke-per-shoe-style-encoding
**Location:** Appendix L.3; Appendix L.6 "Example encoding schemes" (Shoe 00 and Shoe 10; capacity is three levels, NEGATIVE / NEUTRAL / HOT, per hand); Appendix L.7
**Grep command used:** `/usr/bin/grep -n "bespoke per shoe|asks a question  on any topic|complains about the  weather|is  functional" literature/extracted/rose_2026.txt`
