## Claim 2: Board moves versus language — a move is the action, but many token sequences realise one decision

> "However, the “game of natural language” differs from board games in a key respect: in board games, a move is already the action, whereas in language, many surface forms realize the same decision."

> "For LLM-based reasoning, treating actions as raw token strings therefore overcounts branching by semantic aliasing—paraphrases, formatting variants, and tool-call surface forms can occupy different branches while expressing nearly identical intent"

> "We therefore take the position that token sequences are an inconvenient interface for search and learning, while backbone hidden states provide a more faithful representation of state and policy."

**Location:** Section 1; Figure 1 caption ("Go versus language games as action spaces", citing Wittgenstein's builder–assistant example)
