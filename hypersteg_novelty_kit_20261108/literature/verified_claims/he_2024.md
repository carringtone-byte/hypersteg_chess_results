## Claim 1: Approach — re-train transformer encoders as Hierarchy Transformer encoders (HiTs) in a Poincaré ball with curvature set by dimension

> "To address this, we introduce a novel approach to re-train transformer encoder-based LMs as Hierarchy Transformer encoders ( HiT s), harnessing the expansive nature of hyperbolic space. Our method situates the output embedding space of pre-trained LMs within a Poincaré ball with a curvature that adapts to the embedding dimension, followed by training on hyperbolic clustering and centripetal losses."

> "These losses are designed to effectively cluster related entities (input as texts) and organise them hierarchically."

## Claim 3: Geometry fitted to the encoder — a tanh output hyper-cube circumscribed by a Poincaré ball of radius √d, with no added parameters

> "transformer encoder-based LMs typically use a  tanh \tanh  activation function in the last layer, which maps each embedding dimension to the range"

> "Thus, we can construct a Poincaré ball of radius  d \sqrt{d}  (or equivalently, curvature value  c = 1 d c=\frac{1}{d} ) whose boundary circumscribes the hyper-cube."

> "It is also worth mentioning that these changes do not add in learnable parameters besides the ones already in LMs, thus retaining the original architectures of LMs as encoders."

> "Unlike previous hyperbolic embedding methods that utilise the entire hyperbolic space and often require a projection layer to manage out-of-manifold embeddings, our method ensures that embeddings are contained within a specific subset of this manifold."

## Claim 4: Losses — a hyperbolic triplet clustering loss, and a centripetal loss that puts parents nearer the origin, the origin acting as an imaginary root

> "This loss aims at clustering related entities and distancing unrelated ones in the Poincaré ball. We formulate it in the form of triplet loss because related entities are not equivalent but their semantic distances should be smaller than those between unrelated entities."

> "This loss ensures parent entities are positioned closer to the Poincaré ball’s origin than their child counterparts, reflecting the natural expansion of hierarchies from the origin to the boundary of the manifold."

> "is used to imply that the manifold’s origin represents an  imaginary root entity  for everything."
